3 · OUR RESEARCH QUESTION
Does Size, Neighborhood, and Timing All Decide a Home's Price?
To answer that, we tested three hypotheses:
H1The bigger the home — more bedrooms, more square footage — the higher the price.But some homes are smartly designed — more bedrooms without much extra square footage.
H2Zip codes with better-built homes (higher average grade) also carry higher prices.But even an average-grade zip code might already offer a good price.
H3There are specific months — likely winter — when prices are lower than the rest of the year.
HYPOTHESIS 1
Does Size Really Decide the Price?
HYPOTHESIS
The bigger the home — more bedrooms, more square footage — the higher the price.But some homes are smartly designed — more bedrooms without much extra square footage.
What we looked at: living space, bedrooms, bathrooms, and price.
HYPOTHESIS 1 · FINDING 1 OF 9
Living space and price move closely together — a correlation of 0.70 out of 1, making square footage one of the strongest single drivers of price.
HYPOTHESIS 1 · FINDING 2 OF 9
Median price increases with bedroom count, then levels off past 5 bedrooms.
HYPOTHESIS 1 · FINDING 3 OF 9
Median price keeps climbing with more bathrooms — bathrooms track price even more closely than bedrooms.
HYPOTHESIS 1 · FINDING 4 OF 9
1–2 bedroom homes cost $334–$389 per square foot, while 4–6 bedroom homes cost just $239–$248 — bigger is actually cheaper per square foot.
HYPOTHESIS 1 · FINDING 5 OF 9
Cost per square foot holds steady or even rises with more bathrooms, so bathrooms are worth paying for.
HYPOTHESIS 1 · FINDING 6 OF 9
Price jumps at every size tier — Small $329K, Medium $445K, Large $673K, Luxury $1.05M — with the biggest jump past 2,500 sq ft.
HYPOTHESIS 1 · FINDING 7 OF 9
Cost efficiency improves sharply once a home has 4 or more bedrooms — that's the sweet spot for space per dollar.
HYPOTHESIS 1 · FINDING 8 OF 9
A finished basement adds real value in 3–4 bedroom homes, but that gap disappears by 5 bedrooms.
HYPOTHESIS 1 · FINDING 9 OF 9
Many 4-bedroom homes fit at or below 2,200 sq ft, while 5-bedroom homes jump toward 3,000 sq ft.
HYPOTHESIS 1 · CONCLUSION
Smart Layout Beats Sprawling Luxury
$415K  vs  $715K
Smart layout (4+ beds, ≤ 2,400 sq ft) vs. sprawling luxury (≤ 3 beds, 2,500+ sq ft)
Save $300,000
a 42% discount for the same bedroom count
HYPOTHESIS 2
Does a Nicer Neighborhood Cost More?
HYPOTHESIS
Zip codes with better-built homes (higher average grade) also carry higher prices.But even an average-grade zip code might already offer a good price.
What we looked at: zip code, construction grade, and median price.
HYPOTHESIS 2 · FINDING 1 OF 2
Build quality and price move together closely (r = 0.82) — better-built zip codes cost more.
HYPOTHESIS 2 · FINDING 2 OF 2
Standard zip codes median $305K, Above Average $442K (+45%), Premium $710K — "Above Average" is the value sweet spot.
HYPOTHESIS 2 · CONCLUSION
Above Average Zip Codes Are the Sweet Spot
$305K  →  $442K  →  $710K
Standard grade  →  Above Average grade (+45%)  →  Premium grade — median price by zip code quality
Target "Above Average" (7.3–8.0 grade)
a nice, well-kept area — without the luxury markup of top-tier zip codes
HYPOTHESIS 3
Is There a Best Time to Buy?
HYPOTHESIS
There are specific months — likely winter — when prices are lower than the rest of the year.
What we looked at: sale date, average price per month, and number of sales per month.
HYPOTHESIS 3 · FINDING 1 OF 2
February has the lowest average price of the year (~$508K) — the cheapest month to buy.
HYPOTHESIS 3 · FINDING 2 OF 2
January and February also have the fewest sales — less competition alongside the lowest prices.
HYPOTHESIS 3 · CONCLUSION
Winter Is the Buyer's Window
February
The lowest average price of the year (~$508K) — and one of the quietest months for sales
House-hunt in January–February
the lowest prices, paired with the least competition from other buyers
